Go Nonna does not have a product or experience problem: it matches or beats every direct competitor in Long Island City on rating. The problem is visibility. It competes against restaurants with up to 3.5 times more reviews, and its most distinctive asset — being the only place in the area combining Italian and Argentine cooking — goes almost entirely uncommunicated.

I audited its Google Business Profile, website, Instagram and Facebook, and benchmarked the business against five comparable Italian restaurants nearby. I identified three priority opportunities, all executable without ad spend.

My role: I worked at Go Nonna as a server for an extended period, which gave me direct insight into how the restaurant operates and who its diners are. I carried out this audit independently, alongside my digital marketing training, without access to internal accounts or sales data. The analysis draws exclusively on public, observable information.

The market gap

Every competitor reviewed competes on the same claim: "authentic Italian." None occupies the Italian-Argentine space, or the neighbourhood-trattoria angle. That is the differentiated value proposition Go Nonna already owns and isn't using.

Three priorities

1. Incorrect categories on the Google Business Profile

Problem — The profile is classified under categories that don't describe the business, diluting its relevance in local searches.

Evidence — Alongside Italian and Argentine restaurant, the profile is listed as "shipping service" and "diner."

Proposal — Clean up the category list, keeping only the accurate ones. Standardise a single address across all platforms.

Metric — Monthly searches surfacing the profile, and local ranking for "italian restaurant Long Island City."

2. The differentiator and promotions are only communicated sporadically

Problem — The most distinctive element of the business goes unstated, and promotions are announced once and then disappear.

Evidence — No competitor reviewed offers both Italian and Argentine cooking, yet most channels present Go Nonna simply as "Italian." The happy hour was posted once with no recurrence, and appears neither on the website nor on Google.

Proposal — State the Italian-Argentine proposition in the bio, homepage and Google description. Turn the happy hour into recurring communication: fixed weekly post, permanent highlight, site mention, Google update post.

Metric — Website clicks and direction requests; covers during the happy hour window before and after.

3. Social content volume far below potential

Problem — With fewer than a hundred posts total, social channels generate almost no discovery among new customers.

Evidence — Roughly 75 posts and 1,416 followers since 2019, no short-form video. Customers tag the restaurant on their own without that content being reused.

Proposal — Reshare every customer tag to stories; two short unedited videos per week; mirror Facebook automatically.

Metric — Reach among non-followers, and customer mentions per month.
